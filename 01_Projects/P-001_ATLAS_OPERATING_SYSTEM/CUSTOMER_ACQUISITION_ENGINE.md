**Document ID:** customer_acquisition_engine.md  
**Project:** P-001 Atlas Operating System  
**Status:** Active  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Owner:** Антон  
**Purpose:** Convert validated market opportunities into real prospects, customers, revenue and repeatable acquisition systems.

---

## 1. Purpose

Atlas must not stop at identifying attractive markets.

A market opportunity becomes materially more valuable when Atlas can identify:

- who the potential customers are;
- where they can be found;
- whether they have the relevant problem;
- whether the problem is urgent;
- whether they are willing to pay;
- how they can be reached;
- whether they respond;
- whether they will test the product;
- whether they will pay;
- whether they can become repeat customers;
- whether additional customers can be acquired economically.

The objective is therefore:

**Market → Customer Discovery → Customer Acquisition → Validation → Revenue → Repeatability → Automation → Scale**

Atlas should continuously improve this system using real customer behavior.

---

## 2. Core Principle

Atlas does not consider an opportunity validated because:

- the market is large;
- competitors exist;
- people say the idea is interesting;
- social media discusses the trend;
- AI makes the product technically possible;
- a landing page receives traffic;
- people sign up for a free trial.

The strongest evidence is:

**A qualified customer gives time, tests the product, provides information, agrees to a pilot, and ultimately pays.**

Customer behavior should therefore be weighted more heavily than theoretical market attractiveness.

Every validated opportunity should be processed through:

**Opportunity**
→ **ICP Definition**

→ **Prospect Identification**

→ **Retention**
→ **Expansion**
→ **Referral**
→ **Repeatable Acquisition**

Atlas should measure conversion between every stage.

## 4. Ideal Customer Profile

For every opportunity Atlas must define an ICP before large-scale outreach begins.

The ICP should include:

- industry;
- company size;
- geography;
- revenue range where relevant;
- employee count;
- decision-maker role;
- existing software/tools;
- operational characteristics;
- likely pain points;
- urgency;
- ability to pay;
- purchasing behavior;
- technical maturity;
- regulatory constraints;
- estimated customer lifetime value.

The ICP must be specific enough that Atlas can actually find individual companies matching it.

Bad ICP:

> Small businesses that need AI.

Good ICP:

> UK-based dental clinics with 3–10 locations, 10–80 employees, active appointment scheduling, high inbound call volume and an identifiable administrative workload.

---

## 5. Customer Problem Definition

Atlas must identify the actual customer problem before designing the acquisition campaign.

For each opportunity answer:

1. What problem exists?
2. Who experiences it?
3. How frequently does it occur?
4. How expensive is it?
5. How much time does it consume?
6. What does the customer currently use?
7. What happens if the problem is not solved?
8. Is the problem urgent?
9. Has the customer already attempted to solve it?
10. Does the customer currently spend money on the problem?

Preference should be given to problems that already generate expenditure.

Existing spending is strong evidence of willingness to pay.

---

## 6. Customer Readiness Score

Every opportunity should receive a Customer Readiness Score.

Evaluate:

- current demand;
- urgency;
- existing spending;
- willingness to pay;
- frequency of the problem;
- accessibility of customers;
- ease of demonstrating value;
- competitive saturation;
- switching difficulty;
- purchasing friction.

Score each factor from 1–10.

The system should distinguish:

### BUILD

Strong evidence of current demand and willingness to pay.

### WATCH

Interesting opportunity with promising signals but insufficient evidence.

### AVOID

Weak demand, low willingness to pay, excessive competition, poor timing or difficult customer acquisition.

## 7. Customer Discovery

Atlas should actively search for evidence of customer pain.

Potential sources include:

- search behavior;
- marketplaces;
- business directories;
- public company websites;
- industry communities;
- Reddit;
- forums;
- social media;
- reviews;
- app reviews;
- job postings;
- customer complaints;
- support discussions;
- competitor reviews;
- competitor pricing;
- product comparison websites;
- public case studies;
- procurement activity;
- existing software adoption.

The objective is not simply to count mentions.

Atlas should identify evidence that customers are actively trying to solve the problem.

## 8. Prospect Discovery

Once the ICP is defined, Atlas should identify real prospects.

For every prospect record:

- company name;
- website;
- industry;
- location;
- company size;
- estimated revenue where available;
- relevant decision-maker;
- role;
- public professional profile;
- public business contact information;
- existing solution;
- identified pain signal;
- qualification score;
- source;
- date discovered;
- acquisition status.

Only lawful and appropriately available business information should be used.

Atlas should avoid unnecessary collection of sensitive personal information.

## 9. Prospect Qualification

Not every prospect deserves outreach.

Each prospect receives a qualification score.

Suggested criteria:

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---:|
| ICP fit | 20% |
| Problem relevance | 20% |
| Problem urgency | 15% |
| Existing spending | 15% |
| Ability to pay | 10% |
| Decision-maker accessibility | 10% |
| Timing signal | 10% |

Total:

**100 points**

Suggested classification:

- **80–100:** High-priority prospect
- **65–79:** Medium-priority prospect
- **50–64:** Low-priority prospect
- **Below 50:** Do not prioritize

The scoring system should improve as real conversion data accumulates.

---

## 10. Buying Signals

Atlas should search for observable signals that indicate increased probability of purchase.

Examples:

- hiring related personnel;
- opening new locations;
- rapid company growth;
- adopting adjacent software;
- publicly discussing the problem;
- negative reviews related to the problem;
- searching for solutions;
- requesting recommendations;
- launching a new service;
- increasing advertising;
- changing suppliers;
- regulatory changes;
- operational expansion;
- existing use of competing products;
- dissatisfaction with existing solutions.

Buying signals should increase prospect priority.

---

## 11. Contact Strategy

Atlas should determine the most appropriate acquisition channel for each opportunity.

Possible channels:

- email;
- LinkedIn;
- website contact forms;
- inbound landing pages;
- partnerships;
- marketplaces;
- communities;
- referrals;
- direct sales;
- paid acquisition;
- content;
- SEO;
- affiliate channels;
- reseller channels.

Atlas should not assume that one acquisition channel works for every market.

The cheapest effective acquisition channel should receive priority.

---

## 12. Outreach Strategy

Outreach should be based on the customer's problem rather than the product's features.

Bad:

> We built an AI automation platform with 25 features.

Better:

> We noticed your team handles a large volume of appointment requests manually. We are testing a system that may reduce that workload. Would you be open to testing it?

Outreach should be:

- relevant;
- concise;
- specific;
- personalized where economically justified;
- easy to respond to;
- focused on the customer's problem;
- respectful of applicable laws and platform rules.

Atlas should avoid spam-oriented behavior.

---

## 13. Personalization Economics

Personalization has a cost.

Atlas should determine whether personalization increases conversion enough to justify its cost.

Measure:

**Incremental Conversion Gain / Incremental Acquisition Cost**

High-value prospects may justify deep personalization.

Low-value prospects may require standardized messaging.

Atlas should therefore optimize:

**Conversion × Customer Value ÷ Acquisition Cost**

rather than maximizing personalization alone.

---

## 14. Outreach Experiments

Atlas should treat outreach as an experiment.

For each campaign record:

- ICP;
- offer;
- channel;
- message;
- number of prospects;
- number contacted;
- delivery rate;
- response rate;
- positive response rate;
- qualification rate;
- meeting rate;
- pilot rate;
- payment rate;
- revenue;
- acquisition cost;
- time spent.

The system should compare experiments rather than relying on intuition.

---

## 15. Response Classification

Responses should be classified automatically where possible.

Suggested categories:

- positive;
- interested;
- asks for more information;
- asks for pricing;
- wants a demo;
- wants a pilot;
- not now;
- already has solution;
- not relevant;
- negative;
- unsubscribe / do not contact;
- referral;
- unknown.

Positive responses should trigger the next appropriate workflow.

Negative or opt-out responses must be respected.

---

## 16. Lead Qualification

Atlas should determine whether an interested prospect is worth pursuing.

Questions include:

- Does the problem actually exist?
- How important is it?
- How often does it occur?
- What does it cost the customer?
- What solution do they currently use?
- Who makes the purchasing decision?
- What is the expected budget?
- How quickly do they want a solution?
- What would prevent adoption?
- What would make them test the product?

The objective is to avoid spending excessive time on prospects with low purchasing probability.

---

## 17. Pilot Conversion

When appropriate, Atlas should convert qualified prospects into low-friction pilots.

A pilot should define:

- customer;
- problem;
- starting condition;
- expected outcome;
- duration;
- scope;
- success metric;
- price;
- responsibilities;
- next decision.

The pilot should answer:

**Does the product create enough value for the customer to pay?**

A free pilot should not automatically be preferred.

Where possible, Atlas should test willingness to pay early.

---

## 18. Payment Validation

Payment is one of the strongest validation signals.

Atlas should distinguish:

- interest;
- registration;
- trial;
- pilot;
- paid pilot;
- subscription;
- one-time payment;
- repeat purchase;
- expansion.

A customer saying:

> "This is a great idea"

is weak evidence.

A customer saying:

> "Send me the invoice"

is strong evidence.

## 20. CAC Model

Atlas should calculate Customer Acquisition Cost.

Basic formula:

**CAC = Total Acquisition Cost / New Paying Customers**

Acquisition cost should include, where applicable:

- advertising;
- software;
- data;
- outreach infrastructure;
- sales labor;
- contractor costs;
- commissions;
- customer acquisition experiments.

Time should also be tracked.

A business with high monetary margin but extremely high human acquisition effort may still be unattractive.

---

## 21. LTV Model

Atlas should estimate Customer Lifetime Value.

Simplified model:

**LTV = Average Revenue per Customer × Gross Margin × Expected Customer Lifetime**

Where sufficient data exists, Atlas should use actual cohort behavior rather than theoretical lifetime assumptions.

LTV should be treated as uncertain until enough customer data exists.

---

## 22. Acquisition Economics

Atlas should evaluate:

**LTV / CAC**

and:

**CAC Payback Period**

A theoretically attractive business should be downgraded if customer acquisition is too expensive or too slow.

A smaller market may be more attractive if customers are significantly easier and cheaper to acquire.

---

## 23. Automation Levels

Customer acquisition should gradually move through:

### Level 1 — Manual

Human performs research, prospecting and outreach.

### Level 2 — Assisted

Atlas performs research and generates recommendations.

### Level 3 — Semi-Automated

Atlas finds prospects, scores them and prepares outreach.

### Level 4 — Automated

Atlas executes approved acquisition workflows within defined limits.

### Level 5 — Adaptive

Atlas continuously analyzes results and improves targeting, messaging and channel allocation.

Automation should increase only after the process demonstrates reliable economics.

---

## 24. Human Approval Gates

Atlas should not automatically perform high-risk actions without appropriate controls.

Human approval should be required when appropriate for:

- major spending;
- unusual campaigns;
- sensitive industries;
- legal or regulatory uncertainty;
- high-volume outreach;
- changes to pricing;
- major contractual commitments;
- actions that could materially damage reputation.

The objective is:

**Maximum automation without sacrificing control.**

**Better Product**
→ **Better Customer Outcome**
→ **Higher Conversion**
→ **More Customers**
→ **More Data**
→ **Better Targeting**
→ **Lower CAC**
→ **Higher Profit**
→ **More Reinvestment**

Atlas should actively search for opportunities where this flywheel can develop.

## 26. Acquisition Channel Ranking

Atlas should continuously rank channels according to:

- conversion rate;
- CAC;
- time to customer;
- scalability;
- repeatability;
- margin;
- operational complexity;
- platform dependency;
- risk.

A channel should receive more capital when evidence shows improving economics.

---

## 27. Customer Concentration Risk

Atlas should monitor customer concentration.

A business should not be considered highly robust when:

- one customer produces an excessive percentage of revenue;
- one platform controls distribution;
- one acquisition channel supplies nearly all customers;
- one partnership determines the economics.

Atlas should identify concentration risk before scaling.

---

## 28. Retention

Acquisition without retention is not a strong business.

Atlas should measure:

- activation;
- usage;
- repeat purchase;
- renewal;
- churn;
- customer complaints;
- expansion;
- referrals.

Where retention is weak, Atlas should investigate the product before increasing acquisition spending.

---

## 29. Referral Engine

Once customers demonstrate satisfaction, Atlas should test referrals.

Potential mechanisms:

- referral incentives;
- partner programs;
- customer introductions;
- case studies;
- testimonials;
- affiliate arrangements;
- reseller relationships.

Referral acquisition should be compared against paid and outbound acquisition.

## 30. Customer Feedback Intelligence

Track:

- objections;

- reasons for purchase;
- reasons for rejection;

This information should influence:

---

## 31. Opportunity Kill Rules

Atlas should reduce or stop acquisition investment when:

- qualified prospects consistently reject the problem;
- willingness to pay is weak;
- response rates remain poor after meaningful experimentation;
- conversion does not improve;
- CAC becomes structurally unattractive;
- retention is poor;
- the market is excessively saturated;
- acquisition depends on unsustainable manual effort;
- regulatory or platform risk becomes excessive.

A failed acquisition experiment is useful information.

The objective is not to protect every opportunity.

The objective is to protect capital.

---

## 32. Opportunity Expansion Rules

Atlas should increase allocation when evidence demonstrates:

- strong customer demand;
- repeated positive responses;
- increasing conversion;
- successful paid pilots;
- attractive gross margins;
- acceptable CAC;
- strong retention;
- repeatability;
- scalable acquisition;
- low operational complexity.

Capital should follow evidence.

---

## 33. Cross-Opportunity Learning

Atlas should not treat every business as completely independent.

The system should learn:

- which customer types buy;
- which industries respond;
- which channels work;
- which messages work;
- which price points convert;
- which objections repeat;
- which acquisition methods produce the lowest CAC.

Knowledge from one opportunity should improve future opportunity evaluation where applicable.

## 34. Customer Acquisition Score

Every opportunity should receive an Acquisition Score.

Suggested weighting:

- ICP clarity — 10%
- Customer accessibility — 15%
- Problem urgency — 15%
- Willingness to pay — 20%
- Response probability — 10%
- Conversion potential — 10%
- CAC potential — 10%
- Retention potential — 5%
- Scalability — 5%

Total:

**100 points**

The score is not permanent.

It should change as evidence accumulates.

---

## 35. Atlas Acquisition Decision

Atlas should classify acquisition opportunities as:

### SCALE

Strong evidence of customer demand, conversion and economics.

### TEST

Promising but insufficient evidence.

### ITERATE

Customers exist but the offer, positioning, channel or economics need improvement.

### HOLD

Potentially attractive but timing or evidence is insufficient.

### KILL

Evidence does not support further acquisition investment.

## 37. Core Atlas Principle

Atlas does not merely search for businesses.

Atlas searches for:

**Problems → Customers → Willingness to Pay → Repeatable Acquisition → Profitable Economics**

The ultimate objective is to create a system that can repeatedly discover opportunities, find customers, validate demand, generate revenue and build increasingly automated businesses.

The system should become better with every experiment.

1. Define the ICP.
2. Identify 50–100 real prospects.

4. Identify appropriate decision-makers.

7. Run a controlled acquisition test.
8. Measure response rate.
9. Measure positive response rate.
10. Measure qualified conversation rate.
11. Measure pilot conversion.
12. Measure payment conversion.

## 39. Final Rule

Atlas should never ask only:

**"Can we build it?"**

Atlas must ask:

**"Can we find the customers?"**

**"Can we reach them?"**

**"Will they respond?"**

**"Will they try it?"**

**"Will they pay?"**

**"Will they stay?"**

**"Can we acquire more customers profitably?"**

**"Can the acquisition process itself be automated?"**

Only when the answers become increasingly positive should Atlas move from experiment to business.
