# Atlas Customer Discovery Engine

**Document ID:** customer_discovery_engine.md  
**Project:** P-001 Atlas Operating System  
**Status:** Active  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Owner:** Антон  

**Purpose:** Identify, segment, score and validate real potential customers for an opportunity before significant capital, product development or scaling.

---

## 1. Purpose

Atlas should not evaluate an opportunity only by asking:

> "Is there a market?"

Atlas must also determine:

- who the actual customers are;
- whether those customers have the relevant problem;
- how urgent the problem is;
- whether customers are actively looking for a solution;
- what solutions they use today;
- how much they currently spend;
- whether they are willing to switch;
- whether they are willing to pay;
- how they can be reached;
- whether they respond to outreach;
- whether they will test the product;
- whether they will continue using it;
- whether additional customers can be acquired economically;
- whether customer acquisition can eventually be automated.

The objective is to convert market assumptions into customer evidence.

---

## 2. Core Principle

Atlas does not treat theoretical market size as proof of business viability.

A large market with weak customer willingness to pay may be inferior to a smaller market with:

- strong pain;
- clear urgency;
- existing spending;
- easy customer identification;
- accessible decision makers;
- short sales cycles;
- strong retention;
- repeat purchases;
- low acquisition cost.

Therefore:

**Customer evidence > market size alone.**

---

## 3. Customer Discovery Sequence

For every opportunity Atlas should follow this sequence:

**Market → Segment → Customer profile → Pain → Existing behavior → Buying trigger → Reachability → Response → Trial → Payment → Retention → Repeat purchase → Acquisition economics → Automation potential**

An opportunity should not progress simply because the market appears attractive.

Each stage should produce evidence for the next stage.

---

## 4. Customer Segment Definition

Atlas should identify the narrowest practical initial customer segment.

The initial segment should define:

- industry;
- company size;
- geography;
- business model;
- approximate revenue;
- number of employees;
- decision maker;
- operational role;
- technology maturity;
- current workflow;
- relevant pain;
- current solution;
- estimated spending;
- buying authority.

Avoid vague definitions such as:

> "small businesses"

Prefer definitions such as:

> "US-based dental clinics with 3–10 employees using online appointment scheduling and receiving a high volume of repetitive patient inquiries."

The more precisely the customer can be identified, the easier customer acquisition becomes.

## 6. Customer Pain Evaluation

Atlas should distinguish between:

### A. Nice-to-have

The product improves something but the customer can comfortably continue without it.

### B. Useful

The product creates meaningful value but the customer may postpone purchase.

### C. Painful

The customer experiences a recurring problem and actively wants improvement.

### D. Urgent

The problem creates measurable financial, operational, regulatory, time or competitive pressure.

Atlas should prioritize C and D.

---

## 7. Existing Customer Behavior

Atlas should investigate what customers already do.

Questions:

- What do customers currently use?
- Do they solve the problem manually?
- Do they pay another company?
- How much do they pay?
- How much employee time does the problem consume?
- How frequently does the problem occur?
- What happens if they do nothing?
- Have they recently searched for alternatives?
- Have they recently changed providers?
- Are competitors already selling solutions?
- Are customers requesting similar solutions publicly?

Existing spending and behavior should receive more weight than hypothetical interest.

---

## 8. Willingness To Pay

Atlas must distinguish:

**interest ≠ intent ≠ trial ≠ payment.**

Evidence should be ranked approximately as follows:

### Strong evidence

- customer pays;
- customer signs;
- customer prepays;
- customer starts a paid pilot;
- customer replaces an existing paid solution;
- customer commits budget.

### Medium evidence

- customer agrees to a pilot;
- customer provides data or access;
- customer requests pricing;
- customer schedules a sales call;
- customer asks implementation questions.

### Weak evidence

- customer says the idea is interesting;
- survey response;
- social-media engagement;
- likes;
- comments;
- generic positive feedback.

Atlas should never treat weak evidence as equivalent to revenue.

---

## 9. Why Would The Customer Buy Now?

For every opportunity Atlas should answer:

> **Why would a customer buy this now?**

Possible buying triggers include:

- rising costs;
- labor shortages;
- new regulation;
- increased competition;
- lost revenue;
- increasing customer demand;
- operational bottleneck;
- new technology adoption;
- expensive manual work;
- existing software becoming inadequate;
- seasonal demand;
- major industry change;
- measurable ROI;
- urgent business problem.

If no convincing current trigger exists, reduce the opportunity score.

---

## 10. Market Readiness

Atlas should classify customer demand as:

### BUILD

Strong current demand + credible willingness to pay + reachable customers.

### WATCH

Promising signals but insufficient evidence.

### AVOID

Weak willingness to pay, excessive saturation, poor timing, inaccessible customers or unattractive acquisition economics.

A technically possible product should not automatically become a BUILD opportunity.

---

## 11. Customer Accessibility

Atlas must evaluate how easily potential customers can be identified and reached.

Evaluate:

- public company databases;
- websites;
- directories;
- marketplaces;
- LinkedIn;
- professional communities;
- social media;
- industry associations;
- public contact information;
- inbound search demand;
- advertising platforms;
- partnerships;
- referrals;
- existing platforms.

The goal is not merely to find customers.

The goal is to determine whether customers can be acquired **repeatably and economically**.

---

## 12. Customer Acquisition Channel

For every opportunity Atlas should identify the most promising acquisition channels.

Possible channels:

- cold email;
- LinkedIn;
- direct messages;
- search;
- SEO;
- paid advertising;
- marketplaces;
- partnerships;
- affiliates;
- communities;
- outbound calling;
- referrals;
- content;
- existing customer referrals;
- platform integrations.

Atlas should compare channels instead of assuming one channel will work.

---

## 13. Outreach Validation

Before scaling acquisition, Atlas should run a controlled outreach experiment.

Measure:

- prospects identified;
- contacts reached;
- messages delivered;
- replies;
- positive replies;
- meetings booked;
- trials started;
- paid customers;
- revenue generated;
- acquisition cost;
- time required.

The objective is to measure actual customer behavior.

---

## 14. Customer Response Score

Atlas should score customer response using actual behavior.

Example:

| Signal | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Paid customer | 10 |
| Paid pilot | 9 |
| Signed commitment | 9 |
| Trial started | 7 |
| Meeting booked | 6 |
| Positive reply | 5 |
| Pricing request | 5 |
| Detailed product request | 4 |
| Generic interest | 2 |
| Social engagement | 1 |
| No response | 0 |

These weights are starting guidelines, not permanent rules.

Actual observed conversion rates should eventually replace assumptions.

---

## 15. Customer Discovery Experiment

Every opportunity should have a cheapest credible customer-validation experiment.

The experiment should answer:

1. Can customers be identified?
2. Can decision makers be reached?
3. Will they respond?
4. Do they acknowledge the problem?
5. Will they test a solution?
6. Will they pay?
7. Will they continue?
8. Can more customers be acquired economically?

The cheapest experiment should be preferred before expensive development.

---

## 16. Customer Acquisition Economics

Atlas should estimate:

- customer acquisition cost;
- average revenue per customer;
- gross margin;
- payback period;
- expected lifetime value;
- retention;
- churn;
- sales-cycle length;
- labor required per acquisition;
- channel scalability.

Basic principle:

**A customer is not necessarily valuable simply because they pay.**

The acquisition process must also produce attractive economics.

---

## 17. Automation Potential

Atlas should evaluate whether customer acquisition can eventually be automated.

Evaluate each stage:

1. Prospect identification.
2. Prospect qualification.
3. Contact enrichment.
4. Message generation.
5. Personalization.
6. Outreach.
7. Follow-up.
8. Response classification.
9. Lead qualification.
10. Meeting scheduling.
11. Proposal generation.
12. Trial onboarding.
13. Payment.
14. Customer onboarding.
15. Retention.
16. Upsell.
17. Referral generation.

Each stage should be classified:

- manual;
- semi-automated;
- automatable;
- fully automatable;
- requires human approval.

---

## 18. Human-in-the-Loop Rule

Atlas should automate repetitive processes but should not automatically perform high-risk actions without appropriate controls.

Human approval may be required for:

- unusual customer requests;
- contracts;
- large financial commitments;
- refunds;
- sensitive communications;
- regulated industries;
- legal commitments;
- reputation-sensitive outreach.

Automation should increase throughput without removing necessary judgment.

---

## 19. Customer Acquisition Flywheel

A validated opportunity should gradually develop the following loop:

**Find prospects → qualify prospects → contact prospects → receive responses → qualify leads → run trials → convert customers → retain customers → generate referrals → improve targeting → reduce CAC → automate acquisition → increase volume**

The objective is to make customer acquisition increasingly repeatable.

---

## 20. Customer Evidence Hierarchy

Atlas should rank evidence approximately in this order:

1. Revenue.
2. Repeat revenue.
3. Paid pilot.
4. Customer retention.
5. Multiple paying customers.
6. Trial conversion.
7. Qualified sales pipeline.
8. Positive customer responses.
9. Customer interviews.
10. Search demand.
11. Competitor activity.
12. Surveys.
13. Social-media attention.
14. Founder assumptions.

Actual behavior should generally outweigh opinions.

---

## 21. Customer Concentration Risk

Atlas should evaluate whether the opportunity depends on a small number of customers.

Measure:

- percentage of revenue from largest customer;
- number of potential customers;
- number of reachable customers;
- customer diversification;
- geographic concentration;
- industry concentration.

A business requiring one or two customers for survival should receive a higher concentration-risk score.

---

## 22. Retention Test

Atlas should not evaluate acquisition without considering retention.

Questions:

- Will the customer continue using the product?
- How frequently is the problem experienced?
- Does the product become embedded in workflow?
- Is switching away inconvenient?
- Does the customer receive recurring value?
- Is there a reason to renew?
- Is there an opportunity for expansion?

A business with easy acquisition but poor retention should not automatically be considered attractive.

---

## 23. Repeat Purchase Potential

Atlas should evaluate:

- subscriptions;
- recurring services;
- repeat transactions;
- usage-based revenue;
- upsells;
- cross-sells;
- renewals;
- referrals.

Recurring or repeatable revenue should generally receive a higher score than purely one-time revenue, all else equal.

---

## 24. Customer Discovery Score

Each opportunity should receive a Customer Discovery Score from 0–100.

Suggested dimensions:

- Customer pain: 15
- Urgency: 10
- Existing spending: 10
- Willingness to pay: 15
- Customer accessibility: 10
- Response rate: 10
- Trial conversion: 10
- Retention potential: 5
- Acquisition economics: 10
- Automation potential: 5

Total:

**100 points**

These weights should be treated as a starting framework.

Atlas should update the weighting system as empirical evidence accumulates.

---

## 25. Acquisition Readiness

Each opportunity should be classified as:

### NOT READY

Insufficient evidence that customers can be identified, reached or converted.

### DISCOVERY

Customers can be identified but willingness to pay is not yet validated.

### VALIDATION

Customers respond and demonstrate meaningful interest or trial behavior.

### COMMERCIAL

Customers pay and acquisition economics are becoming measurable.

### SCALE

Customer acquisition is repeatable, retention is acceptable and unit economics support additional capital.

---

## 26. Kill Rules

Atlas should stop or pause an opportunity when:

- customers cannot be reached;
- decision makers are inaccessible;
- response rates remain persistently weak;
- customers acknowledge no meaningful problem;
- customers refuse to pay;
- acquisition costs are structurally too high;
- retention is poor;
- sales cycles are too long;
- the market is excessively saturated;
- the product requires disproportionate manual work;
- the opportunity depends on unrealistic assumptions.

The objective is not to save every idea.

The objective is to preserve capital and attention for opportunities with evidence.

## 28. Full-Cycle Customer Validation

Atlas should attempt to validate the complete chain:

**Problem → Customer → Reach → Response → Trial → Payment → Retention → Repeat Purchase → Economic Acquisition → Automation**

A market opportunity becomes substantially stronger when multiple stages are validated simultaneously.

---

## 29. Core Atlas Principle

Atlas should not ask only:

> "Is there a market?"

Atlas should ask:

> "Who will buy?"

> "Why will they buy?"

> "Why will they buy now?"

> "Can we find them?"

> "Can we reach them?"

> "Will they respond?"

> "Will they try the product?"

> "Will they pay?"

> "Will they stay?"

> "Can we acquire more customers profitably?"

> "Can the acquisition process itself be automated?"

Only when the answers become increasingly positive should Atlas move from research to validation and from validation to business creation.

---

## 30. Final Rule

**Atlas does not build businesses based only on market opportunity.**

Atlas builds businesses where:

**Current Demand + Customer Willingness to Pay + Reachable Customers + Positive Acquisition Evidence + Attractive Economics + Retention + Automation Potential**

create a credible path to a scalable business.

The ultimate objective is not merely to discover profitable markets.

The objective is to build a system that can repeatedly:

**find opportunities → find customers → validate demand → acquire customers → generate revenue → automate acquisition → scale winners → reinvest into the next opportunity.**
