## 1. Investment Thesis

Atlas will investigate whether a small, highly specialized AI automation service can solve a repetitive business process for small and medium-sized businesses and generate recurring revenue.

The initial objective is NOT to build a large software company.

The initial objective is to prove that a business will pay for a measurable result produced by an AI-assisted workflow.

If the service demonstrates demand, positive unit economics and repeatability, Atlas may gradually transform the service into a standardized product and eventually software.

## 4. Core Problem

Many SMBs lose money because:

- inquiries are answered slowly;
- leads are not followed up;
- employees perform repetitive tasks;
- information is entered manually;
- appointments are missed;
- customer requests are not categorized;
- managers spend time on low-value administrative work.

The opportunity is to automate a measurable portion of this workflow.

---

## 5. Initial Offer

The initial offer should be outcome-based.

Example:

> "We install an AI-assisted system that responds to incoming customer inquiries, qualifies the customer, records the lead and automatically follows up."

The offer should focus on:

**money saved + leads recovered + time saved + response speed**

rather than on the technology itself.

## 9. Success Criteria

### Strong signal

Any of the following:

- 1+ paying pilot;
- multiple businesses request a demo;
- repeated pain is confirmed across the same niche;
- customers accept a recurring pricing model;
- customers quantify meaningful time or revenue savings.

### Very strong signal

- 3+ paying customers;
- repeatable acquisition;
- positive gross margin;
- standardized delivery;
- clear recurring revenue.

---

## 10. Failure Criteria

Consider Kill / Hold / Pivot when:

- 20–30 qualified prospects produce no meaningful interest;
- the problem is not financially important;
- customers refuse to pay;
- implementation requires excessive custom work;
- gross margin appears structurally poor;
- customer acquisition cost is excessive;
- the workflow cannot be reliably automated;
- owner involvement remains too high.

Failure of this experiment does not mean AI automation is rejected globally.

The specific niche, offer or workflow may simply be wrong.
