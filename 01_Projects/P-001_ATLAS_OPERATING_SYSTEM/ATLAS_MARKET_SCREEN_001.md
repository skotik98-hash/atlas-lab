## 9. Capital Allocation Principle

During the experimental phase:

- minimize initial capital;
- maximize information gained per dollar;
- validate demand before significant development;
- increase capital only after evidence;
- reinvest profits into proven winners;
- maintain a strategic reserve;
- take only small owner dividends during growth.

Capital allocation must follow demonstrated performance.

---

## 10. Validation Rules

An opportunity should progress when it demonstrates:

- real customer demand;
- willingness to pay;
- attractive unit economics;
- repeatability;
- scalability;
- automation potential.

An opportunity should be held or killed when evidence does not support further capital allocation.

## 12. Crypto Treasury

Crypto is not excluded from the Atlas portfolio.

Atlas may use crypto assets as a treasury allocation when the expected risk-adjusted return is attractive and liquidity remains sufficient.

Crypto opportunities must be separated into distinct categories:

### 12.1 Native Staking

Examples:
- ETH
- SOL
- ADA
- other established proof-of-stake assets

Evaluate:
- staking yield;
- token price volatility;
- liquidity;
- validator / network risk;
- lock and unbonding conditions;
- fees;
- inflation / token issuance;
- historical drawdowns;
- expected return in USD/EUR terms.

### 12.2 Liquid Staking

Evaluate separately from native staking.

Additional risks:
- smart contract risk;
- liquid staking protocol risk;
- depeg risk;
- liquidity risk.

### 12.3 DeFi Yield

Higher-risk category.

Evaluate:
- protocol revenue;
- liquidity;
- smart contract risk;
- counterparty exposure;
- leverage;
- liquidation risk;
- historical failures;
- sustainability of yield.

### 12.4 Crypto Businesses

Examples:
- crypto analytics;
- automation;
- monitoring;
- infrastructure;
- SaaS;
- research tools.

These are evaluated as businesses rather than investments.

### 12.5 Speculative Crypto

Examples:
- trading bots;
- arbitrage;
- new protocols;
- highly speculative tokens.

Speculative crypto receives a separate risk classification and must never be confused with treasury capital.

---

## 13. Treasury Rule

Free Atlas capital may temporarily be allocated to income-producing assets when:

- liquidity remains sufficient;
- the allocation does not prevent funding a validated opportunity;
- expected risk-adjusted return is attractive;
- risks are explicitly documented.

Staking is therefore treated as a potential capital-management tool, not as a substitute for building profitable businesses.

Capital allocation must remain dynamic and follow demonstrated opportunity quality.

---

## 14. First Crypto Research

The first crypto treasury screen should compare established staking opportunities.

For each candidate evaluate:

1. Current staking yield.
2. Expected net yield.
3. Token price volatility.
4. Historical maximum drawdown.
5. Liquidity.
6. Lock / unbonding conditions.
7. Validator and network risks.
8. Smart-contract risks where applicable.
9. Inflation and token issuance.
10. Expected return in USD/EUR.
11. Worst-case scenario.
12. Recommended Atlas allocation.

No capital should be committed solely because the nominal staking APY is attractive.
